KING LEAR:
What say you to the world, and the state of the world,
And the world is not the father to the state,
And that the world hath made the world to be,
And that the son of the king of England's crown,
Which is the cause of all this heavy grief.
Then speak, my lord, and let me hear the truth:
What is the matter that you come to me?

GLOUCESTER:
My lord, the Duke of Cornwall and his duchess
Are here arrived, and with them comes the king,
Whose heart is broken with his daughters' words.
I would not have him know of this revolt,
For he is full of sorrow and despair.

KING LEAR:
Thou hast not seen me yet: I will not stay
To hear thee speak. Go, call my daughter hither;
For in this stormy night the heavens do frown
Upon our miserable heads. What, Kent! Where is the fool?
Let me not stay a jot for dinner: go, get it ready.
How now! Daughter, what makest thou here?
Dost thou come with such a look of cold disdain
To strike thy father's blessing from his lips?

REGAN:
Sir, I am glad to see your highness well,
Though grieved to hear of your distempered state.
Nature in you stands on the very verge
Of her confine: you should be ruled and led
By some discretion that discerns your state
Better than you yourself. Therefore, I pray you,
That to our sister you make due return;
Say you have wronged her.
